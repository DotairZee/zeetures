# Research behind the creatures

Zeetures's creatures run on an original mind and an original set of behaviours, not a recreation of the Creatures 2 brain. These are the published works and sources that informed them. Each entry says what it informed. The list is also shown in the game, under Help > About.

## Inspiration

- Grand, S. (2000). *Creation: Life and How to Make It.* Weidenfeld & Nicolson. The general approach: creatures as whole artificial organisms with drives, chemistry and learning.
- Grand, S., Cliff, D., & Malhotra, A. (1997). Creatures: Artificial life autonomous software agents for home entertainment. *Proceedings of the First International Conference on Autonomous Agents.* The original game's design, read for its concepts, not copied.

## The mind (drives, learning and choice)

- Cannon, W. B. (1932). *The Wisdom of the Body.* Norton. Homeostasis: needs as departures from a set point.
- Hull, C. L. (1943). *Principles of Behavior.* Appleton-Century. Drive reduction as reward.
- Keramati, M., & Gutkin, B. (2014). Homeostatic reinforcement learning for integrating reward collection and physiological stability. *eLife, 3*, e04811. [Link](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.04811) The overall drive, in which a severe need outweighs several mild ones, and reward as the drop in it.
- Rescorla, R. A., & Wagner, A. R. (1972). A theory of Pavlovian conditioning. In A. H. Black & W. F. Prokasy (Eds.), *Classical Conditioning II* (pp. 64-99). Appleton-Century-Crofts. Learning what each action does (the delta rule).
- Sutton, R. S., & Barto, A. G. (2018). *Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction* (2nd ed.). MIT Press. [Link](http://incompleteideas.net/book/the-book-2nd.html) Learning from reward, and exploring by how little something has been tried.
- Berlyne, D. E. (1960). *Conflict, Arousal, and Curiosity.* McGraw-Hill. Curiosity, and boredom raising it.
- Berridge, K. C., & Robinson, T. E. (1998). What is the role of dopamine in reward: hedonic impact, reward learning, or incentive salience? *Brain Research Reviews, 28*(3), 309-369. Wanting (what a creature expects) kept apart from liking (what it gets).
- Ludlow, A. R. (1976). The behaviour of a model animal. *Behaviour, 58*, 131-172. Sticking with an action rather than dithering.
- McFarland, D. J., & Sibly, R. M. (1975). The behavioural final common path. *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B, 270*, 265-293. Choosing one action among competing needs.
- Tyrrell, T. (1993). *Computational Mechanisms for Action Selection.* PhD thesis, University of Edinburgh. Action selection in artificial animals.
- Blumberg, B. M. (1996). *Old Tricks, New Dogs: Ethology and Interactive Creatures.* PhD thesis, MIT Media Laboratory. Ethology for interactive creatures.
- Thompson, R. F., & Spencer, W. A. (1966). Habituation: A model phenomenon for the study of neuronal substrates of behavior. *Psychological Review, 73*(1), 16-43. Habituation: losing interest in what changes nothing.
- Rankin, C. H., et al. (2009). Habituation revisited. *Neurobiology of Learning and Memory, 92*(2), 135-138. [Link](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2008.09.012) The same, updated.
- Russell, J. A. (1980). A circumplex model of affect. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 39*(6), 1161-1178. Mood as valence and arousal.

## Temperament and relationships

- Temperament (seven traits, each a continuous score, loosely linked as animal personalities are) and relationships (bonds from time together and mating, grooming and food as social currency, harm weighing more than help) were drawn from general reading on animal personality and bonding. The specific sources were not recorded at the time.

## Scents

- Karlson, P., & Lüscher, M. (1959). "Pheromones": a new term for a class of biologically active substances. *Nature, 183*, 55-56. What a pheromone is.
- Wilson, E. O., & Bossert, W. H. (1963). Chemical communication among animals. *Recent Progress in Hormone Research, 19*, 673-716. Releasers (quick) and primers (slow). [Overview](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2014.00231/full)
- Wyatt, T. D. (2010). Pheromones and signature mixtures. *Journal of Comparative Physiology A, 196*, 685-700. [Link](https://pherobase.com/literature/data/details/C/51866) Species-wide scents against each creature's own scent.
- Ant trails: Argentine ants choosing the shorter bridge by positive feedback. [Scientific American, 2008](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/swarm-smarts-2008-02/)
- Pharaoh's ants: random foraging in small colonies, trails above a size. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55516) And a "no entry" scent at unrewarding forks. [University of Sussex](https://sro.sussex.ac.uk/id/eprint/22787/)
- Evaporation as forgetting in ant colony optimisation. [Paper](https://pleiad.cl/_media/bic2007/papers/inspiration-optimization.pdf)
- Bonabeau, E., Theraulaz, G., & Deneubourg, J.-L. Fixed response thresholds and the regulation of division of labor. [Santa Fe Institute](https://www.santafe.edu/research/results/working-papers/fixed-response-thresholds-and-the-regulation-of-di) Each creature its own threshold for responding.
- Alarm scents: aphids habituating to an alarm pheromone [J. Chem. Ecol.](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10886-008-9528-x); one leafcutter ant compound attracting at low strength and alarming at higher [USDA](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/22401); a signal's "active space" [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5875169)
- Necromones: harvester ants carrying oleic acid-treated paper to the refuse pile. [Psyche](https://groups.csail.mit.edu/mac/projects/psyche/65/65-108.html)
- Mammals: mouse urine effects on oestrus, puberty and pregnancy (Whitten, Vandenbergh and Bruce effects) [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-13933-3_3); boar saliva and the sow's mating stance [Animal Science](https://resolve-he.cambridge.org/core/journals/animal-science/article/pig-courtship-behaviour-pheromonal-property-of-androstene-steroids-in-male-submaxillary-secretion/08672A832131D225346604BBB7288126); a rabbit milk compound and newborn nipple search [PhilArchive](https://dc2.philarchive.org/rec/LUOGMA); human pheromones and menstrual synchrony contested [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25740891/)
- Following a scent: moths surging upwind and casting when they lose it, Vickers & Baker (1994), *PNAS* [PDF](https://ento.psu.edu/files/91vickers-baker1994pnas.pdf); recently mated male moths ignoring female scent for a night, *Journal of Experimental Biology* [Link](https://cob.silverchair.com/jeb/article-split/213/7/1100/10126/Mating-induced-transient-inhibition-of-responses)

## Seasons

- Henningsen, J. B., Gauer, F., & Simonneaux, V. (2016). A review of RFRP neurons as the link between melatonin and reproduction in seasonal breeders. *Frontiers in Endocrinology, 7*, 36. [Link](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fendo.2016.00036/full) Breeding timed by day length; long-day and short-day breeders. Informed the spring breeding season.
- Seasonal breeding and photoperiod, a review. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6017951) The same.
- Human birth seasonality: the peak birth month moving from March to August in West Germany as couples gained control over timing (2024) [Journal of Perinatal Medicine](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/jpm-2024-0526/html?lang=en); conception odds best in late autumn (2020) [Boston University](https://www.bu.edu/sph/2020/02/18/late-fall-may-be-best-time-of-year-to-try-to-conceive)
- Campbell, L. A. D., et al. (2018). Social thermoregulation as a potential mechanism linking sociality and fitness: Barbary macaques with more social partners form larger huddles. *Scientific Reports, 8*, 6074. [Link](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5904170/) Company keeping creatures warm in winter. Informed winter bonds.
- Huddling across mammals, and how much energy it saves (Brandt's voles). [ScienceDaily, 2018](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/05/180530113118.htm) The same, and small animals gaining most.
- Loneliness by season in people: a very small winter peak (PASST study poster, 2024) [Poster](https://conference2.aau.at/event/324/contributions/1668/contribution.pdf); a pilot study of loneliness over shorter periods (University of Kent, 2015) [PDF](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/12038/1/Fulltext.pdf) The human evidence is weak and mixed; winter bonds rest mainly on the animal work.
- Madden, P. A., Heath, A. C., Rosenthal, N. E., & Martin, N. G. (1996). Seasonal changes in mood and behavior: the role of genetic factors. *Archives of General Psychiatry, 53*(1), 47-55. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8540777/) Seasonality partly inherited. Informed the inherited seasonal rhythm.
- An overview of epidemiological studies on seasonal affective disorder. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10721866/) Seasonal shifts in mood, energy, sleep and appetite; more rest in winter.
- A review of seasonal aggression and its hormonal control. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6980702) Breeding-season rivalry (not built).
- Food caching in autumn: British Trust for Ornithology [BTO](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/behaviour/caching-food); hoarding in animals [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoarding_(animal_behavior)) Informed the autumn appetite (storing as fat rather than caching).
- Play in juvenile Belding's ground squirrels and later boldness. *Journal of Mammalogy, 98*(4). [Link](https://bioone.org/journals/journal-of-mammalogy/volume-98/issue-4/gyx049/Play-behavior-and-responses-to-novel-situations-in-juvenile-ground/10.1093/jmammal/gyx049.full) Informed summer play, strongest in the young.
- Nelson, R. J., & Demas, G. E. (1996). Seasonal changes in immune function. *Quarterly Review of Biology, 71*(4), 511-548. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8987173/) Immunity and breeding traded off by season (not built).
- Weil, Z. M., Borniger, J. C., Cisse, Y. M., Abi Salloum, B. A., & Nelson, R. J. (2015). Neuroendocrine control of photoperiodic changes in immune function. *Frontiers in Neuroendocrinology, 37*, 108-118. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4402123) The same.
