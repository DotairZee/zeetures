# Research behind Zeetures

Zeetures's creatures run on an original mind and an original set of behaviours, not a recreation of the Creatures 2 brain. These are the published works and sources that informed them. Each entry says what it informed. Where an older work is listed, a "Since then" line gives more recent research on the same idea. The list is also shown in the game, under Help > About.

## Inspiration

- Grand, S. (2000). *Creation: Life and How to Make It.* Weidenfeld & Nicolson. The general approach: creatures as whole artificial organisms with drives, chemistry and learning.
- Grand, S., Cliff, D., & Malhotra, A. (1997). Creatures: Artificial life autonomous software agents for home entertainment. *Proceedings of the First International Conference on Autonomous Agents.* The original game's design, read for its concepts, not copied.

## The mind (drives, learning and choice)

- Cannon, W. B. (1932). *The Wisdom of the Body.* Norton. Homeostasis: needs as departures from a set point.
  - Since then: Ramsay, D. S., & Woods, S. C. (2014). Clarifying the roles of homeostasis and allostasis in physiological regulation. *Psychological Review, 121*(2), 225-247. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4166604) Regulation also anticipates needs (allostasis), not only corrects them. Informed: the autumn appetite, and eating ahead of need with food at hand when dusk, a sleep or scarcity is coming.
- Hull, C. L. (1943). *Principles of Behavior.* Appleton-Century. Drive reduction as reward.
  - Since then: drive reduction as the whole of reward is superseded; the mind's modern basis is Keramati & Gutkin (2014, below) and Berridge & Robinson's wanting and liking (below).
- Keramati, M., & Gutkin, B. (2014). Homeostatic reinforcement learning for integrating reward collection and physiological stability. *eLife, 3*, e04811. [Link](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.04811) The overall drive, in which a severe need outweighs several mild ones, and reward as the drop in it.
- Cabanac, M. (1971). Physiological role of pleasure. *Science, 173*, 1103-1107. [Link](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.173.4002.1103) Alliesthesia: how pleasant something is depends on the body's state; a sweet taste is less pleasant after a sugar load. Informed: food less pleasant the fuller a creature is, so full creatures stop eating for fun.
  - Since then: Berridge, K. C. (1991). Modulation of taste affect by hunger, caloric satiety, and sensory-specific satiety in the rat. *Appetite, 16*, 103-120. [PDF](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/2027.42/29389/1/0000460.pdf) And sensory-specific satiety (Rolls & Rolls), a decline in the pleasantness of a food once eaten [Overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensory-specific_satiety); a 2021 review of oro-sensory satiety [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8143001)
- Rescorla, R. A., & Wagner, A. R. (1972). A theory of Pavlovian conditioning. In A. H. Black & W. F. Prokasy (Eds.), *Classical Conditioning II* (pp. 64-99). Appleton-Century-Crofts. Learning what each action does (the delta rule).
  - Since then: Schultz, W. (2016). Dopamine reward prediction error coding. *Dialogues in Clinical Neuroscience, 18*(1), 23-32. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4826767) The same prediction error, found in dopamine neurons.
- Sutton, R. S., & Barto, A. G. (2018). *Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction* (2nd ed.). MIT Press. [Link](http://incompleteideas.net/book/the-book-2nd.html) Learning from reward, and exploring by how little something has been tried.
- Berlyne, D. E. (1960). *Conflict, Arousal, and Curiosity.* McGraw-Hill. Curiosity, and boredom raising it; at first, things worth trying for being little known.
  - Since then: Kidd, C., & Hayden, B. Y. (2015). The psychology and neuroscience of curiosity. *Neuron, 88*(3), 449-460. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4635443) And Gottlieb, J., & Oudeyer, P.-Y. (2018). Towards a neuroscience of active sampling and curiosity. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience.* [Link](https://dx.doi.org/10.1038/s41583-018-0078-0) And Oudeyer, P.-Y., Gottlieb, J., & Lopes, M. (2016). Intrinsic motivation, curiosity, and learning: theory and applications in educational technologies. *Progress in Brain Research, 229*, 257-284. [PDF](https://www.pyoudeyer.com/oudeyerGottliebLopesPBR16.pdf) Curiosity as information seeking, drawn to what one is still learning (learning progress), not to novelty alone. Informed: since 2026-11-16 a creature's curiosity, after its first few tries of a thing, follows how fast it is still learning about it.
- Berridge, K. C., & Robinson, T. E. (1998). What is the role of dopamine in reward: hedonic impact, reward learning, or incentive salience? *Brain Research Reviews, 28*(3), 309-369. Wanting (what a creature expects) kept apart from liking (what it gets).
  - Since then: Berridge, K. C., & Robinson, T. E. (2016). Liking, wanting, and the incentive-sensitization theory of addiction. *American Psychologist, 71*(8), 670-679. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5171207) The distinction has held up.
- Ludlow, A. R. (1976). The behaviour of a model animal. *Behaviour, 58*, 131-172. Sticking with an action rather than dithering.
- McFarland, D. J., & Sibly, R. M. (1975). The behavioural final common path. *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B, 270*, 265-293. Choosing one action among competing needs.
- Tyrrell, T. (1993). *Computational Mechanisms for Action Selection.* PhD thesis, University of Edinburgh. Action selection in artificial animals.
- Blumberg, B. M. (1996). *Old Tricks, New Dogs: Ethology and Interactive Creatures.* PhD thesis, MIT Media Laboratory. Ethology for interactive creatures.
  - Since then: (action selection, with Ludlow, McFarland & Sibly and Tyrrell above) Dunovan, K., & Verstynen, T. (2016). Believer-skeptic meets actor-critic: rethinking the role of basal ganglia pathways during decision-making and reinforcement learning. *Frontiers in Neuroscience, 10*, 106. [Link](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnins.2016.00106/full) How the brain commits to one action and weighs exploring against exploiting.
- Thompson, R. F., & Spencer, W. A. (1966). Habituation: A model phenomenon for the study of neuronal substrates of behavior. *Psychological Review, 73*(1), 16-43. Habituation: losing interest in what changes nothing.
- Rankin, C. H., et al. (2009). Habituation revisited. *Neurobiology of Learning and Memory, 92*(2), 135-138. [Link](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2008.09.012) The same, updated.
  - Since then: McDiarmid, T. A., Yu, A. J., & Rankin, C. H. (2019). Habituation is more than learning to ignore: multiple mechanisms serve to facilitate shifts in behavioral strategy. *BioEssays, 41*(9), e1900077. [Link](https://doi.org/10.1002/bies.201900077) Habituation as a shift of strategy, not only fading interest.
- Russell, J. A. (1980). A circumplex model of affect. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 39*(6), 1161-1178. Mood as valence and arousal.
  - Since then: Barrett, L. F., & Bliss-Moreau, E. (2009). Affect as a psychological primitive. *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology, 41*, 167-218. [PDF](https://www.affective-science.org/pubs/2009/barrett-blissmoreau-2009.pdf) And a 2024 review of valence and arousal research in *Motivation and Emotion*, noting how calm, pleasant states are neglected. [PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11031-024-10062-5.pdf) Valence and arousal still the standard description of mood.

## Reading the game's genomes

- Ashton, M. (1999). *Creatures 2 CAOS Language Guide.* Creature Labs (now Gameware Development). The documented meanings of the genome's brain cells: the sense lobe's 32 situations, the drive and decision lobes, and the object categories. Informed how the genomes' sense-, drive- and decision-cell emitters and their instincts are read, without running the original brain.
- Lummox JR (1999). *Nova Subterra* genome for Creatures 2, with its author's notes ("Genetic Overview", "Search and Sub-Attention Lobes", "Afterdrive and Change Lobes"). What its four added lobes are for. Informed the roles Nova Subterra's lobes get (brain-profiles.txt).
- Morris, L., & Double, C. *The Canny Norns for C2* (genome v1.2), with its authors' notes. What the echo, search and choice lobes are for, and that Canny norns are "much more curious than a normal norn". Informed the roles Canny's lobes get and its curiosity setting (brain-profiles.txt).

## Temperament

Researched on 2026-11-11 for the seven inborn traits. Several entries were read from abstracts and summaries rather than the full papers.

- Thomas, A., & Chess, S. The New York Longitudinal Study: nine temperament dimensions, the "easy", "difficult" and "slow-to-warm-up" types, and "goodness of fit" between temperament and surroundings. [Summary](https://courses.lumenlearning.com/suny-lifespandevelopment/chapter/temperament/) [Encyclopedia](https://www.encyclopedia.com/medicine/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/temperament) Informed: every creature having some of every trait, and temperament mattering through how it meets the world.
  - Since then: Shiner, R. L., Buss, K. A., McClowry, S. G., Putnam, S. P., Saudino, K. J., & Zentner, M. (2012). What is temperament now? Assessing progress in temperament research. *Child Development Perspectives, 6*(4), 436-444. [Record](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/what-is-temperament-now-assessing-progress-temperament-research-o/) Where the four classic temperament theories (Thomas and Chess, Rothbart and others) stand today.
- Rothbart, M. K. The Infant Behavior Questionnaire (IBQ-R): surgency, negative affectivity and effortful control. [Instrument](https://docs.hbcdstudy.org/r1.1/instruments/bcgi/ibqr/) [Rothbart lab](https://research.bowdoin.edu/rothbart-temperament-questionnaires/?p=64) Informed: energy, reactivity and steadiness.
- Kagan, J. Behavioural inhibition: about one infant in five "high reactive", fearful later on, with only moderate stability. [Harvard Gazette, 2010](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2010/04/often-we-are-what-we-were/) [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2789287) Informed: boldness and reactivity.
- Saudino, K. J. (2005). Behavioral genetics and child temperament. *Journal of Developmental & Behavioral Pediatrics, 26*(3), 214-223. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1188235) Temperament only partly heritable (about 0.2 to 0.6). With Dochtermann below, informed the inherited temperament's heritability of 0.4.
- Réale, D., Reader, S. M., Sol, D., McDougall, P. T., & Dingemanse, N. J. (2007). Integrating animal temperament within ecology and evolution. *Biological Reviews, 82*(2), 291-318. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17437562/) Informed: the traits themselves (shyness-boldness, exploration, activity, aggressiveness, sociability became boldness, curiosity, energy, temper, sociability).
- Sih, A., Bell, A. M., Johnson, J. C., & Ziemba, R. E. (2004). Behavioral syndromes: an integrative overview. *Quarterly Review of Biology, 79*(3), 241-277. [Link](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/422893) Informed: traits loosely linked (bold creatures tending to be curious, active and a little quicker to anger).
- Gosling, S. D., & John, O. P. (1999). Personality dimensions in nonhuman animals: a cross-species review. *Current Directions in Psychological Science, 8*(3), 69-75. [Link](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/current-directions/1467-8721.00017/) Which personality dimensions hold across species.
  - Since then: Wolf, M., & Weissing, F. J. (2012). Animal personalities: consequences for ecology and evolution. *Trends in Ecology & Evolution, 27*(8), 452-461. [PDF](https://www.rug.nl/research/gelifes/tres/publications/_pdf/woWeTREE12.pdf) What personality differences do for populations, with Réale (2007) and Sih (2004) above.
- Bell, A. M., Hankison, S. J., & Laskowski, K. L. (2009). The repeatability of behaviour: a meta-analysis. *Animal Behaviour, 77*(4), 771-783. [PDF](https://alisonbelllab.web.illinois.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Bell-et-al-2009-repeatability.pdf) About a third of behaviour is a stable individual trait; informed how strongly traits shift settings.
- Dochtermann, N. A., Schwab, T., & Sih, A. (2015). The contribution of additive genetic variation to personality variation: heritability of personality. *Proceedings of the Royal Society B, 282*, 20142201. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25961374/) About half of animal personality variation genetic.
- Bierbach, D., Laskowski, K. L., & Wolf, M. (2017). Behavioural individuality in clonal fish arises despite near-identical rearing conditions. *Nature Communications, 8*, 15361. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5442312) Informed: temperament drawn at hatching, regardless of genome.
- Freund, J., et al. (2013). Emergence of individuality in genetically identical mice. *Science, 340*, 756-759. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23661762/) The same.
- Buchanan, S. M., Kain, J. S., & de Bivort, B. L. (2015). Neuronal control of locomotor handedness in *Drosophila*. *PNAS, 112*(21), 6700-6705. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4450378) Lifelong individual quirks that are not inherited. The same.
- Ayroles, J. F., et al. (2015). Behavioral idiosyncrasy reveals genetic control of phenotypic variability. *PNAS, 112*(21), 6706-6711. [Link](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/behavioral-idiosyncrasy-reveals-genetic-control-of-phenotypic-var/) How variable individuals are is itself heritable.
- Roberts, B. W., & DelVecchio, W. F. (2000). The rank-order consistency of personality traits from childhood to old age. *Psychological Bulletin, 126*(1), 3-25. [Summary](https://stafforini.com/works/roberts-2000-rankorder-consistency-personality/) Traits settling with age.
  - Since then: Bleidorn, W., et al. (2022). Personality stability and change: a meta-analysis of longitudinal studies. *Psychological Bulletin, 148*(7-8), 588-619. [Link](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000365) Stability rises through early life and levels off in young adulthood.
- Differential susceptibility ("orchids and dandelions"): some individuals are shaped more by their surroundings, for better and worse. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2834322/) [UC Davis](https://ccfs.ucdavis.edu/sites/g/files/dgvnsk841/files/inline-files/Orchid%20or%20Dandelion.pdf) Considered; not built.

## Relationships

Researched on 2026-11-11 for bonds between creatures (familiarity, liking, fear, friends, enemies and mates, each kept per direction). Several entries were read from abstracts and summaries.

- Aragona, B. J., & Wang, Z. (2004). The prairie vole: an animal model for behavioral neuroendocrine research on pair bonding. *ILAR Journal, 45*(1), 35-45. [PDF](https://psy.fsu.edu/~wanglab/PDF-papers/2004/04ILAR.pdf) Informed: pair bonds from mating and time together.
  - Since then: Walum, H., & Young, L. J. (2018). The neural mechanisms and circuitry of the pair bond. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 19*, 643-654. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6283620/) A bond as the partner linked with reward, as bonds grow here from help felt.
- Montoya, R. M., Horton, R. S., Vevea, J. L., Citkowicz, M., & Lauber, E. A. (2017). A re-examination of the mere exposure effect. *Psychological Bulletin, 143*(5), 459-498. [PubMed](https://www.pubmed.gov/28263645/) Informed: familiarity from time spent together raising liking.
- Dunbar, R. I. M. Grooming, gossip and social bonding in primates. [PDF](https://allegatifac.unipv.it/ziorufus/Dunbar%20gossip.pdf) [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/works/254867) Informed: tickling and petting (grooming) as social currency.
  - Since then: Dunbar, R. I. M. (2018). The anatomy of friendship. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 22*(1), 32-51. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29273112/) Friendship costs time, and networks have a stable size and layers.
- Carter, G. G., & Wilkinson, G. S. (2013). Food sharing in vampire bats: reciprocal help predicts donations more than relatedness or harassment. *Proceedings of the Royal Society B, 280*, 20122573. [Link](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/kip_articles/2082) Informed: food given building a bond.
- Leedale, A. E., et al. (2020). Kin recognition by familiarity and by phenotype matching. *Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution, 8*, 77. [Link](https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fevo.2020.00077/full) The Westermarck effect, co-residence and sexual aversion [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3581061), and the debate over it [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10539-020-09764-0) Informed: close kin and those reared together not mating while an unrelated partner lives.
- Hsu, Y., Earley, R. L., & Wolf, L. L. (2006). Modulation of aggressive behaviour by fighting experience. *Biological Reviews, 81*, 33-74. [Link](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/biological-reviews/article/abs/modulation-of-aggressive-behaviour-by-fighting-experience-mechanisms-and-contest-outcomes/AB01FA1734414B41DC32F2337EF79C24) And de Waal, F. B. M. (2000). Primates: a natural heritage of conflict resolution. *Science, 289*, 586-590. [PDF](https://www.emory.edu/LIVING_LINKS/publications/articles/deWaal_2000b.pdf) Informed: harm adding fear, and fights forgiven in time.
- Harlow's studies of contact comfort, and attachment as a secure base. [UW I-LABS](https://modules.ilabs.uw.edu/module/development-of-attachment/research-with-monkeys/) Informed: the young drawn to those who comfort them.
- Kikusui, T., Winslow, J. T., & Mori, Y. (2006). Social buffering: relief from stress and anxiety. *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B, 361*, 2215-2228. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1764848/) And Hennessy, M. B., Kaiser, S., & Sachser, N. (2009). Social buffering of the stress response. [Summary](https://caninewelfare.centers.purdue.edu/resource/social-buffering-of-the-stress-response-diversity-mechanisms-and-functions) Informed: friends nearby easing fear and stress.
  - Since then: Kiyokawa, Y., & Hennessy, M. B. (2018). Comparative studies of social buffering: a consideration of approaches, terminology, and pitfalls. *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews, 86*, 131-141. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5801062)
- Rusbult, C. E. (1983). A longitudinal test of the investment model. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 45*(1), 101-117. [PDF](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/eli-finkel/documents/13_Rusbult1983_JournalOfPersonalityAndSocialPsychology.pdf) Commitment from rewards, costs and investment.
  - Since then: Le, B., & Agnew, C. R. (2003). Commitment and its theorized determinants: a meta-analysis of the investment model. *Personal Relationships, 10*(1), 37-57. [Record](https://scholarship.haverford.edu/psychology_facpubs/108)
- Gottman's ratio of positive to negative interactions in stable couples. [Gottman Institute](https://www.gottman.com/blog/the-magic-relationship-ratio-according-scienc/) Correlational; with Baumeister below, informed harm weighing more than help.
- Baumeister, R. F., Bratslavsky, E., Finkenauer, C., & Vohs, K. D. (2001). Bad is stronger than good. *Review of General Psychology, 5*(4), 323-370. [Link](https://doi.org/10.1037/1089-2680.5.4.323) Informed: harm shrinking a bond three times as hard as help grows it.
  - Since then: Corns, J. (2018). Rethinking the negativity bias. *Review of Philosophy and Psychology, 9*(3), 607-625. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6132407) The bias is widely accepted but its size hard to pin down; the threefold weight here is a design choice, not a measured ratio.
- Almaatouq, A., et al. (2016). Are you your friends' friend? Poor perception of friendship ties limits the ability to promote behavioral change. *PLoS ONE, 11*(3), e0151588. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4803335) Informed: bonds kept per direction (A's feeling for B is not B's for A).
- Roberts, S. G. B., & Dunbar, R. I. M. (2011). Communication in social networks: effects of kinship, network size, and emotional closeness. *Personal Relationships.* [Record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_miscellaneous_887497992) Informed: bonds fading after long apart, slowest for mates.
- How other games keep relationships, consulted for design: The Sims 2 [StrategyWiki](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/The_Sims_2/Social_Interactions), RimWorld, Dwarf Fortress [wiki](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Friend), and the Creatures series' own brain [Alan Zucconi](https://www.alanzucconi.com/2020/07/27/the-ai-of-creatures/). Informed: two speeds of feeling (recent, and the slow "affinity" meter), and one-sided friendships and grudges.
